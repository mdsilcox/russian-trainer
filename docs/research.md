# Research brief: from B1 Russian to a family trip to Moscow (Sept 2027)

Compiled 2026-10-02 from web research (peer-reviewed SLA studies and verified resources). It informs the grammar reference, feedback design, drills, card format, media shelf and weekly rhythm in this app.

Verification notes: *The Big Silver Book of Russian Verbs* (Jack Franke, McGraw-Hill, 2011) is the real title ("Big Silly" does not exist). No learner podcast called "Russian Progress" could be found; the verified slow-Russian podcast is *Slow Russian* by Daria Molchanova.

## 1. What works for adult language learning (evidence)

| Practice | Finding | Evidence | Source | Implication for 20–30 min/day |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Spaced retrieval** | Spaced reviews beat massed ones; optimal gap grows with retention horizon (≈20–40% of a 1-week horizon, ≈5–10% of a 1-year horizon). | **Strong** | [Cepeda et al. 2008](https://laplab.ucsd.edu/articles/Cepeda%20et%20al%202008_psychsci.pdf) | Keep FSRS; put whole phrases and inflected forms on cards, not just headwords. |
| **Explicit grammar instruction + practice** | Explicit, focused instruction produces large gains and beats implicit (d≈0.73 simple / 0.88 complex forms); gains transfer partly to spontaneous use. | **Strong** (meta-analyses; mostly short-term, controlled measures) | [Norris & Ortega 2000](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/0023-8333.00136); [Spada & Tomita 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9922.2010.00562.x) | Short rule explanation → many quick production drills. Case endings suit this well. |
| **Corrective feedback** | Feedback works and is durable; prompts that make the learner self-correct beat recasts. | **Moderate–strong** | [Lyster & Saito 2010](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/234580927_Oral_Feedback_in_Classroom_SLA_A_Meta-Analysis) | Flag the error and its category, let the learner try again, then reveal the fix. Log errors into drills. |
| **Interleaving** | Mixed grammar practice beat blocked on a 1-week delayed test, despite more errors during practice. | **Moderate** in L2 (strong in general cognitive science) | [Nakata & Suzuki 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/modl.12581) | After a pattern's first introduction, mix cases, motion verbs and aspect in one session. |
| **Extensive reading / comprehensible input** | Extensive reading improves reading rate, comprehension and vocabulary. | **Moderate** (heterogeneous studies) | [Jeon & Day 2016](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1117026.pdf) | Daily easy input at ~95–98% known words. |
| **Vocabulary size & coverage** | ~98% coverage for comfortable comprehension: ≈6–7k word families (speech), 8–9k (text) in English; 95% is a minimum. | **Strong for English; not established for Russian** (inflection complicates counts) | [Nation 2006](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/239928724_How_Large_a_Vocabulary_Is_Needed_for_Reading_and_Listening); [Laufer & Ravenhorst-Kalovski 2010](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ887873.pdf) | Learn by frequency; aim for the top ~3–5k lemmas by Sept 2027. Sharoff et al., *A Frequency Dictionary of Russian* (Routledge; 5,000 words, stress-marked). |
| **Chunks / formulaic language** | More formulaic sequences correlate with rated fluency (r up to ~.60); merely noticing them doesn't stick, deliberate learning does. | **Moderate** (link); **weak** (how to teach) | [Boers & Lindstromberg 2012](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259428229_Experimental_and_Intervention_Studies_on_Formulaic_Sequences_in_a_Second_Language) | SRS whole travel phrases and rehearse them in role-play: *Мо́жно счёт, пожа́луйста?*, *Как дое́хать до…?* |
| **Pronunciation instruction** | Large effect (d≈0.80–0.89); longer programmes and feedback do best. | **Moderate–strong** (86 studies) | [Lee, Jang & Plonsky 2015](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/274178849_The_Effectiveness_of_Second_Language_Pronunciation_Instruction_A_Meta-Analysis) | Stress is trainable: short, regular sessions with feedback. |
| **Shadowing** | Good evidence for listening and fluency; for pronunciation "less systematic and conclusive". | **Moderate** (listening); **weak–moderate** (pronunciation) | [Hamada & Suzuki 2022](https://yuichisuzuki.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Hamada-Suzuki-2022-RELC.pdf); [Whitworth review](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:3104cae1-3a6b-400a-b173-de44384238d2/files/r1z40kv86w) | 5 min, 2–3×/week on short clips with transcript and stress marks. |
| **Time on task** | FSI: ~1,100 class hours for English speakers to reach professional Russian (~B2/C1). Cambridge: ~200 guided hours per CEFR step (180–260 for B1→B2 in English; expect more for Russian). | **Rough guidelines** | [FSI summary](https://www.fsi-language-courses.org/blog/fsi-language-difficulty/); [Cambridge](https://support.cambridgeenglish.org/hc/en-gb/articles/202838506-Guided-learning-hours) | 25 min/day ≈ 150 h/year. Adding 20–30 min/day of easy input makes a strong B1+ / early B2 in practical listening and speaking realistic; full B2 in every skill would need more hours. |

## 2. Russian-specific advice

### Cases: learn by function, prioritise high-frequency forms
- Corpus work (UiT's SMARTool project, Laura Janda's group) shows that for most words 1–3 forms account for nearly all uses, and a learning simulation found training on high-frequency forms worked best. SMARTool is free (~3,000 A1–B2 words with their top forms in context). [SMARTool](https://site.uit.no/clear/2019/11/07/smartool/); [BYU RLJ article](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1316&context=rlj). **Implication:** cards should show common forms in context (*в Москве́*, *из Москвы́*) rather than full paradigms.
- Case errors are the most common grammar error in Russian learner corpora (~14% of all errors, second only to spelling); genitive↔nominative confusion is typical. [Rozovskaya & Roth 2019](https://direct.mit.edu/tacl/article/doi/10.1162/tacl_a_00251/43532/Grammar-Error-Correction-in-Morphologically-Rich)
- The genitive is widely called the "busiest" case (possession, *нет* + noun, quantity, 5+ numerals, many prepositions). Per-case corpus percentages couldn't be verified; treat this ranking as expert consensus.

**Highest-yield targets for travel**
1. **Prepositional after в/на** for location (*в метро́*, *на вокза́ле*, *в гости́нице*), including the stressed -у́ forms (*в аэропорту́*, *в саду́*).
2. **Accusative for direction** (*в Москву́*, *на Кра́сную пло́щадь*) and **animate masculine accusative = genitive** (*ви́жу бра́та*).
3. **Genitive** after *нет / мно́го / ско́лько / из / от / до / у / о́коло / без*, and the **genitive plural** (*пять рубле́й*, *мно́го люде́й*, *биле́тов*), the hardest endings set.
4. **Dative** for *мне ну́жно*, *мне нра́вится*, *к врачу́*, *по у́лице*.
5. **Instrumental** after *с* (*ко́фе с молоко́м*) and *быть / стать / рабо́тать* + profession.
- Learn prepositions as preposition + case pairs: в/на + accusative = direction vs. + prepositional = location; из/с/от + genitive = "from".
- **Numerals:** 1 → nominative singular; 2–4 → genitive singular; 5–20 → genitive plural. Prices, times and ages use this constantly.

### Verbs of motion
Learn as contrasts: unidirectional, now (*идти́, е́хать, лете́ть, нести́*) vs. multidirectional, habitual or round trips (*ходи́ть, е́здить, лета́ть, носи́ть*). Then prefixes, roughly in this order: по- (setting off); при-/у- (arrive/leave); в-/вы- (in/out); пере-, за-, до-, про-, под-/от-. The traditional directionality framing is debated ([Academia.edu article](https://www.academia.edu/114733867/Structuralist_roots_of_the_traditional_instruction_of_Russian_verbs_of_motion_Does_the_directionality_based_approach_fit_to_the_Russian_L2_classroom_)). Travel core: *идти́/ходи́ть, е́хать/е́здить, пойти́/пое́хать, прийти́/прие́хать, вы́йти, перейти́, дое́хать*. Dedicated book: L. Muravyova, *Verbs of Motion in Russian* (Russky Yazyk).

### Aspect
Learn every verb as an aspect pair. Rough default: perfective = a single completed result, often in sequence (*Я купи́л биле́т*); imperfective = process, repetition, "did it happen at all", negated suggestions (*не на́до покупа́ть*), and after *начина́ть / продолжа́ть*. Requests: *Скажи́те, пожа́луйста…* (perfective, specific) vs. *Сади́тесь!* (imperfective, inviting).

### Stress
Stress is lexical and mobile inside paradigms (*рука́ → ру́ку*, *го́род → города́*) but follows a small number of recurring patterns ([elon.io](https://elon.io/grammar/russian/pronunciation/mobile-stress)). English Wiktionary tables mark stress and pattern type; OpenRussian marks stress and flags shifts ([OpenRussian](https://en.openrussian.org/grammar/nouns-declensions)). Show stress marks on every card; learn two forms for nouns whose stress moves. Tool: [stress-russian-books](https://github.com/FreeLanguageTools/stress-russian-books) adds stress marks to e-books.

### Common pitfalls for English speakers
Nominative after numbers and negation; missing animate accusative; в vs. на (*на по́чте*, *на вокза́ле*, *на рабо́те*); dropping *у меня́ есть*; overusing *ты*; *мой* where Russian uses *свой*; *знать / уме́ть / мочь*; overusing pronouns.

## 4. Suggested weekly rhythm (25 min core + optional input)

**Every day (~8 min):** FSRS reviews of phrase cards with stress marks and case forms in context.

| Day | Remaining ~17 min | Optional extra (15–30 min) |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Grammar: one targeted rule → explicit drills from the mistake log | *Russian with Max* episode |
| Tue | Write a story → AI feedback (self-correct first) → log errors | *Easy Russian* with Language Reactor |
| Wed | Interleaved drills (cases + motion + aspect) + 5 min shadowing | Graded reader |
| Thu | Travel role-play (metro, café, pharmacy, tickets, hotel) using chunk cards | *Ку́хня* or a Mosfilm comedy |
| Fri | Translation task + stress drill (mobile-stress nouns, past-tense verbs) | *Slow Russian* |
| Sat | Extensive reading or listening (longer, no drills) | Film with family |
| Sun | Light: review error log, spawn cards, 5 min shadowing | (rest) |

### 12-month arc
- **Oct–Dec 2026, case foundations:** location vs. direction with в/на; genitive (incl. plurals and numbers); animate accusative. Start Mosfilm classics; *Иро́ния судьбы́* at New Year.
- **Jan–Mar 2027, motion and aspect:** unprefixed then prefixed verbs of motion; aspect pairs on every verb card. Move to Olly Richards Intermediate and *Дени́скины расска́зы*.
- **Apr–Jun 2027, fluency and stress:** interleave all weak areas; shadowing and stress drills twice a week; harder role-play (complaints, changing plans, directions you don't fully catch). *Ку́хня*, *Easy Russian*.
- **Jul–Sep 2027, trip rehearsal:** mostly real-world chunks and role-play (transport, metro, ordering, pharmacies, small talk, numbers, prices and times at speed). Keep up Moscow street-speech listening. Lighter new grammar; maintenance SRS only in the last two weeks.

**Realistic expectation:** ~150 core hours plus input time makes a confident B1+ in everyday transactions and noticeably better case accuracy achievable; full B2 across all skills would likely need more hours.
